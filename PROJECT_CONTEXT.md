# GolarionHelper Context

## Goal

Build a local-first Pathfinder 1e character-sheet engine in C++, kept independent from its future portable frontend.

## Current State

- Development is taking place on the orphan branch `cpp-rebuild`.
- The previous Java model rebuild is preserved on `model-rebuild` at commit `e99d776`.
- The project currently contains only a minimal C++20 backend.
- `CharacterSheet` owns and initializes one `ResourceManager` before registering its model components. It exposes rank changes and creation/removal of user-defined skill specializations, plus creation/removal of configured attacks and assignment/removal of their explicit strikes, without model getters or user-facing setters for structural rules. Removing a user-defined specialization unregisters its resource and fails while a structural modifier or skill option still targets it. Canonical specializations cannot be removed.
- `CharacterSheet` owns `Movement` but creates no movement grants. A newly created sheet therefore has no movement mode until its future race, class, effect, or another concrete source registers one; the sheet exposes no user-facing movement mutation.
- Movement uses integer units of 1.5 m and supports land, climb, swim, burrow, and fly modes. Fly grants carry maneuverability (clumsy, poor, average, good, or perfect) and its Pathfinder Fly-check modifier.
- Every movement grant is an independent, atomic profile identified by a stable ID and source. Multiple grants of the same type remain separate alternatives rather than merging speed and properties field by field.
- Each grant registers an enhanceable resource beneath `speed.all`, `speed.<type>`, and `speed.<type>.<grantId>`. Additive speed bonuses therefore reuse normal modifier stacking at global, movement-type, or exact-grant scope.
- `MovementAdjustment` handles non-additive transformations selected globally, by movement type, or by exact grant: percentage speed multipliers, the official reduced-speed table for armor or load, absolute speed limits, maneuverability shifts, and movement blocks. Selectors can require grants whose `affectedByArmor` or `affectedByLoad` flag is true. Armor/load speed reduction is idempotent across multiple sources and is applied after ordinary speed modifiers, while absolute limits remain final. Conditional adjustments are resolved for display but do not alter the permanent result.
- Every movement grant declares whether running with that mode is supported. Components register `RunAdjustment` entries through `movement.runAdjustments`, selected globally, by movement type, or by exact grant. The supported operations are multiplier increase, penalty, replacement, and an explicit block; numeric values are expressions and conditional entries are resolved for display without changing the permanent result.
- The ordinary run multiplier is ×4. Within one semantic stacking group only the highest entry of the same operation applies, allowing heavy armor and a heavy load to impose their shared −1 only once. Increases and penalties from distinct groups combine, active replacements from distinct groups must agree, and any selected block makes running unusable. `RunView` exposes support, usability, base/effective multiplier, final distance, blocking reasons, and complete applied/suppressed provenance for each movement grant.
- A grant cannot be removed while an exact-grant movement/run adjustment or a direct modifier still targets it. Dependents must be removed first, preventing one group from silently mutating another. `MovementView` exposes every independently resolved profile, its modifier snapshot, applicable adjustments, effective speed, maneuverability, usability, and running calculation.
- `CharacterSheet` owns a `SizeManager` with an implicit Medium fallback. The race exclusively owns the optional `SizeBase` entry exposed through `size.base`; a second base is always rejected, even when it declares the same category. Structural effects register `SizeReplacement` and `SizeAdjustment` entries through `size.replacements` and `size.adjustments`; none of these entries are user-authored or persisted. Concurrent replacements must agree, while conflicting results are rejected instead of depending on insertion order.
- Size resolution is declarative: base category, then an optional exact replacement, then the strongest same-direction relative adjustment. Relative adjustments do not stack, opposite directions are rejected as ambiguous, and results are clamped to Fine through Colossal. A replacement whose `acceptsAdjustments` flag is false suppresses but does not remove registered adjustments, so they resume automatically when that replacement ends.
- The Size View exposes base, replacement, selected and actually applied adjustment steps, effective category, every source entry with application state/reason, and the complete universal modifier table. Size is structural state rather than an expression target: whenever the effective category changes, `SizeManager` removes the Modifier instances generated for the previous category and registers new fixed-value size modifiers for melee/ranged attacks and confirmations, armor class, BMC/DMC, Stealth, and Fly. It retains the generated Modifier IDs so cleanup is exact. Attack-mode roots are used deliberately so BMC receives its opposite size modifier exactly once rather than inheriting the ordinary attack-roll modifier as well. Size changes alone do not modify abilities, weapon damage, natural armor, movement, space, or reach; the concrete source remains responsible for non-universal effects.
- `CharacterSheet` owns `CarryingCapacity`, which implements the complete Strength 1–29 metric load table and the `+10 Strength → ×4` progression with checked 64-bit gram arithmetic. It exposes light, medium, and heavy maxima, twice the heavy maximum for lifting from the ground, and five times the heavy maximum for pushing or dragging. Effective Strength is the final `str` target plus the normal Modifier total registered on `carryingCapacity.strength`; zero or lower effective Strength produces zero capacity, and none of the calculated results is registered as an expression target.
- Carrying-capacity size and anatomy are structural inputs. `SizeManager` maintains the single `carryingCapacity.size` entry whenever effective size changes, while the future race exclusively owns the optional `carryingCapacity.bodyType` base entry. With no race entry the model falls back to Bipede; a second body base is rejected. `CarryingCapacity` applies the separate official Bipede/Quadrupede scale for every size from Piccolissima through Colossale. These structural entries are not user-authored or persisted, and `CarryingCapacityView` exposes effective Strength with its Modifier snapshot, effective size, body type/provenance, and every calculated weight threshold.
- Components register source-owned rational `CarryingCapacityMultiplier` entries through `carryingCapacity.multipliers`. Each entry has a stable ID, source, semantic stacking group, and positive numerator/denominator normalized exactly without floating point. Only the highest factor in one stacking group applies, so repeated instances of the same effect do not stack; factors from distinct groups multiply normally. The View exposes the pre-effect thresholds, every applied/suppressed entry with its reason, the reduced combined fraction, and final thresholds. Capacity arithmetic is checked and fractional final grams round down according to the general Pathfinder rounding rule.
- `CharacterSheet` also owns `Encumbrance`, which receives source-owned nonnegative gram contributions through the typed `encumbrance.weights` collection. It sums them with checked 64-bit arithmetic and compares the live total against `CarryingCapacity`, producing Leggero, Medio, Pesante, or Sovraccarico. Weight entries are structural state recreated by their owning equipment/component and are not independently persisted.
- Every load category has one declarative effect profile. Medio applies maximum Dexterity +3, armor check penalty 3, and reduced movement without changing Movement's ordinary ×4 run multiplier. Pesante applies +1, penalty 6, reduced movement, and registers a −1 run adjustment in the semantic group shared with heavy armor. Sovraccarico suppresses the positive AC ability contribution, retains penalty 6, limits load-affected movement to one 1.5 m unit, and registers a global run block. Changing weight or any capacity input removes the old profile's exact effects and applies the new profile before composing a View. `EncumbranceView` exposes thresholds, total and per-source weights, category, its contributed run penalty/block metadata, and whether the total remains within the lift-from-ground and push/drag limits; final run resolution belongs exclusively to `MovementView`.
- Armor-check penalties from armor and weight use the semantic `skill.armorCheckPenalties` collection and the calculated `armorCheckPenalty` target. Every source supplies an expression, `Skills` selects the highest resolved penalty rather than summing candidates, and one aggregate penalty modifier reaches all armor-check-penalty skills through the existing hierarchy. `SkillsView` exposes the total and complete source provenance. A future equipment manager should register the already-combined armor-and-shield candidate as one entry, because armor and shield penalties stack with each other before that equipment result is compared with carried load.
- The equipment domain currently contains its first two value types and the canonical-definition registry. `ItemDefinition` describes a canonical item with stable ID, Italian display name, and nonnegative unit weight in grams. `ItemInstance` has its own stable ID, resolves and retains a stable reference to the singleton-owned definition, and represents one or more interchangeable copies through a strictly positive quantity; definitions are therefore never duplicated per character or instance.
- `ItemDefinitionManager` is a process-wide singleton with no runtime path configuration. The canonical source catalog lives at `core/resources/items.json` and CMake embeds its JSON text into the backend library, making lookup independent from the process working directory and platform asset layout. The first `get()` atomically parses and validates the complete JSON-array catalog into a `std::map`, while subsequent requests return the same stable `const ItemDefinition&`. Definitions cannot be reloaded, removed, or replaced after loading, and duplicate IDs, malformed entries, negative weights, or missing definitions fail explicitly. The first reviewed entry is the standard 15 m hemp rope at 5 kg.
- `ItemInstance::registerWeight()`, `refreshWeight()`, and `unregisterWeight()` add, refresh, and remove the instance's exact `CarriedWeight` through `encumbrance.weights`. Refresh deliberately composes the existing unregister/register operations instead of extending `ResourceManager`; the registered ID is namespaced from the instance ID, the source is the canonical Italian item name, and grams are the checked product of unit weight and current quantity. Duplicate registration, refresh of an absent entry, and removal of an absent entry fail through the existing collection protocol.
- `Inventory` exists as an isolated component and registers the typed `inventory.items` endpoint, but is deliberately not yet owned by `CharacterSheet`: equipped-item transitions and effects must be implemented first. Added instances are uniquely owned by ID, begin at `CarriedItemLocation` in the implicit carried root, and immediately register their complete weight; removal unregisters that exact weight before erasing the instance. Addition rolls back ownership if weight registration fails, duplicate IDs and absent removals fail explicitly, and collection type checking remains centralized in `ResourceManager`. No separate container endpoint exists: later, container capability on a definition will make its stored instance a valid location automatically.
- Equipment placement distinguishes the fourteen canonical humanoid `MagicItemSlot` categories from physical hand occupancy. Every enum has an Italian display name; ring capacity is two and every other magic slot has capacity one, yielding the official fifteen effective positions. `HandSlot` currently represents the humanoid main/off hands, while `HandUsage` describes zero-, one-, or two-hand requirements; the magic `Hands` slot for gloves remains semantically independent from both physical hands.
- `ItemLocation` is the exclusive variant `CarriedItemLocation | ContainerItemLocation | EquippedItemLocation`. Container IDs are normalized and cannot be blank. An equipped location may combine one magic slot with occupied physical hands, supporting a shield, but must contain at least one of those dimensions and cannot repeat a hand. Quantity mutation, actual move/equip operations, slot-capacity validation across instances, container trees and weight policies, equipment effects, Views, and persistence remain outside this slice.
- The six abilities start at 10, validate positive base values, and register an enhanceable resource plus total/modifier targets using `str`/`strMod`, `dex`/`dexMod`, `con`/`conMod`, `int`/`intMod`, `wis`/`wisMod`, and `cha`/`chaMod`.
- Every ability also registers an `ability.<name>.replacements` collection. An `AbilityReplacement` has stable identity and source, an expression, optional requirements, and either the `Base` or `Final` stage. Base replacements replace the saved natural score before normal modifiers, supporting form changes such as a fused Synthesist; when several are active, the highest base value prevails deterministically. Final replacements override the modifier-adjusted result, supporting absolute rules such as Indifeso treating Dexterity as 0; when several are active, the lowest final value prevails. Base replacements must resolve positive and final replacements nonnegative.
- `AbilityView` exposes the natural base, effective base, modifier-adjusted value, final effective value, and complete replacement provenance with requirements, activation, resolved values, and application state. Only the natural base is persisted; structural replacement sources recreate their entries. Existing ability targets resolve the final value, so ability modifiers and every downstream consumer update automatically.
- `BaseAttackBonus` owns the accumulated `baseAttackBonus` resource and exposes its total as the `bab` expression target. It starts at 0, rejects a negative resolved total, has no user setter or persistence data, and will receive structural contributions from character classes and Hit Dice. `CharacterSheetView` includes its total and complete contribution breakdown.
- The strike model separates delivery mode from combinable traits: `AttackMode` distinguishes melee and ranged attack rolls, while `AttackTag` identifies weapon, thrown, projectile, natural, and unarmed strikes without conflating those dimensions. Primary/secondary is not a generic tag: a natural strike carries its required `NaturalAttackClassification`, while non-natural strikes reject it.
- `StrikeGrant` is an immutable structural definition of one complete way to resolve one hit. It has stable identity, provenance, display name, mode, tags, optional natural classification, normalized `usageChannels`, explicit base damage ability plus `DamageAbilityRule`, base critical profile, damage components, reach/range, target defense, and requirements. Usage channels identify the physical resources consumed by the strike, such as `hand.left`, `mouth`, or `tail`; duplicates are rejected, while an empty list remains valid. A grant deliberately contains neither quantity nor iterative sequence; those belong to attack routines. Melee and ranged modes structurally map to Strength and Dexterity only for their default attack-roll ability; damage ability is not inferred from delivery mode.
- A natural grant is registered only once. `StrikeUsage` resolves that same identity as `Default`, `NaturalSecondary`, or `SingleNatural`: secondary use forces the effective classification to secondary, applies the normal -5 attack penalty, and uses half a positive ability modifier for damage; single-natural use attacks at full bonus and uses one-and-a-half times a positive ability modifier. Penalties from a negative modifier remain complete in both cases. `StrikeCalculationContext` accepts normalized per-grant usage overrides and rejects missing grants, duplicate overrides, and non-default natural usages applied to non-natural strikes.
- `Strikes` registers the typed `strike.grants` collection with `ResourceManager` and owns every added grant. For each grant it creates `attack.strike.<id>`, `damage.strike.<id>`, and `criticalConfirmation.strike.<id>` enhanceable resources beneath their applicable mode and tag categories. Removing a grant unregisters all three atomically and is rejected while an exact resource still has a direct modifier.
- `CombatManeuvers` models Bull Rush, Dirty Trick, Disarm, Drag, Grapple, Overrun, Reposition, Steal, Sunder, and Trip independently from concrete attacks. Every maneuver has BMC and CMD resources beneath `combatManeuver.bonus.all` and `combatManeuver.defense.all`; the common BMC resource inherits `attack.all`, because combat maneuver checks are attack rolls.
- BMC is calculated from BAB, Strength by default, and applicable modifiers. Components register `CombatManeuverAbilityReplacement` entries globally or against one maneuver resource; each View contains the fully calculated default and applicable replacement options without persisting a selection. CMD is calculated from 10, BAB, Strength, Dexterity, and its own global/per-maneuver modifiers. `SizeManager` applies the rules' special size values as ordinary `BonusType::Size` bonuses or penalties on the BMC and DMC roots, so they remain visible in the corresponding Modifier views without a dedicated contribution channel.
- `CombatManeuvers` registers the `combatManeuver.defense.dexteritySuppressions` collection resource. Each independently owned entry has a stable ID and source and suppresses only a positive Dexterity modifier from every CMD calculation; a zero or negative modifier remains fully applied. `CombatManeuverDefenseView` exposes the raw modifier, applied modifier, aggregate suppression state, and every active suppression for GUI explanation. This supplies the reversible semantic mechanism required by `Impreparato` without making the condition responsible for combat-maneuver arithmetic.
- `CharacterSheet` owns `Strikes` before `CombatManeuvers`, ensuring the attack resource hierarchy exists before BMC inherits from it. `CharacterSheetView` includes calculated strikes and all ten BMC/CMD results. Weapon-specific maneuver options, selective propagation of eligible AC modifiers, and BAB replacements remain later combat steps.
- `DamageDice` is a validated immutable dice expression with positive dice count and die size. It normally exposes the canonical `XdY` representation; a `d1` result from the weapon-damage progression is displayed as fixed damage. Progression adjustments move by one adjacent step in the canonical Pathfinder table and normalize the documented d4/d10/d12 equivalents, including the special 2d10 transitions. `SizeManager` deliberately does not infer weapon-damage changes: a concrete size-changing source must register the appropriate damage-dice adjustment because Pathfinder effects do not all alter carried equipment in the same way. `DamageType` distinguishes physical, elemental energy, force, positive/negative energy, and untyped hit-point damage; precision, bleed, non-lethal application, materials, alignment, and magical attacks remain separate properties rather than damage types.
- `DamageTypeMode` distinguishes components whose listed types all apply from components that let the user choose one type. Every damage enum value has an Italian `displayName()` for GUI use.
- `DamageComponent` is an immutable identified damage source containing dice, one or more damage types, their all/choice semantics, critical behavior, and optional precision, bleed, or non-lethal traits. It rejects empty or duplicate types, meaningless one-type choices, untyped damage mixed with typed damage, duplicate traits, and precision damage marked as multiplied on a critical hit.
- `StrikeGrant` owns zero or more damage components and requires their IDs to be unique within the strike. Zero components remain valid for strikes that deliver an effect without direct hit-point damage.
- Every damage component has a structural `Base` or `Additional` role. `Strikes` registers the typed `damage.diceAdjustments` collection so independent components can alter matching damage dice through the existing damage resource hierarchy. An adjustment can filter by role, semantic component ID, intrinsic/external origin, and exact external grant ID; duplicate semantic IDs from different grants therefore remain independently addressable. This keeps future size, spell, equipment, and class-feature components independent from `Strikes`.
- Damage-dice adjustments can move by signed steps through the Pathfinder weapon-damage progression, multiply the number of dice, or establish a replacement die expression. Incompatible simultaneous replacements are rejected instead of silently selecting the result with the greatest average. Permanent adjustments produce the default dice; individually conditional results remain available for display, while an attack calculation context applies every currently active condition to the effective dice. The GUI view exposes base/effective dice, conditional results, and complete adjustment provenance.
- Attack distance uses 1.5-metre grid units, matching movement. Melee grants define an optional inclusive minimum/maximum reach interval, including zero reach; ranged grants may define their range increment, maximum number of increments, and penalty for every increment after the first. Thrown attacks normally use five increments and projectile attacks ten, but these remain explicit grant data so exceptional attacks are representable.
- Each distance property has an independent enhanceable hierarchy with global, melee/ranged, attack-tag, and exact-grant resources. Normal modifiers handle additive changes, while entries in the typed `attack.distanceAdjustments` collection apply additive-combination multipliers, lower bounds, or upper bounds. The View distinguishes permanent and context-effective values and retains per-condition previews plus complete provenance. Permanent, individual conditional, and combined active-condition results are all validated as legal reach/range profiles.
- A `StrikeGrant` may carry structural `Requirement` values, while components add or remove external `AttackRequirement` entries through the typed `attack.requirements` collection. External requirements target the normal attack resource hierarchy and may therefore affect all strikes, a mode, a tag, or one exact grant without removing it from the sheet.
- `StrikeView` always retains the strike and exposes its permanent `usable` state, active failure reasons, every evaluated requirement with provenance, and separate usability results for named situational conditions. Conditional requirements do not disable the ordinary strike; they describe whether it is usable when their condition applies. Exact external requirements prevent the owning strike grant from being removed until the dependency is removed.
- Every `StrikeGrant` declares its structural target defense using the existing `ArmorClassType` values: normal, touch, or flat-footed AC. Components register `AttackDefenseReplacement` entries through the typed `attack.defenseReplacements` collection to add alternative defenses for an attack hierarchy target, optionally limited to a named condition such as being within a firearm's first range increment.
- The backend does not select among defense alternatives because target state and distance are encounter inputs. `StrikeView` exposes the base defense first and every applicable replacement with identity, provenance, and condition so the GUI can present the valid comparison. Exact defense replacements prevent removal of their strike grant until the dependency is removed.
- `Strikes` also registers the typed `damage.componentGrants` collection. A `DamageComponentGrant` contributes one external component to every strike whose exact damage resource inherits from its target, allowing effects to address `damage.all`, a mode, a tag, or `damage.strike.<id>` without mutating the immutable `StrikeGrant`. Grant IDs are globally unique and removing a strike is rejected while an external component directly targets it. Calculated strike views merge intrinsic and applicable external components dynamically.
- Critical-confirmation modifiers use a parallel hierarchy rooted at `criticalConfirmation.all`, with mode, tag, and exact-grant resources. An exact confirmation resource also inherits its exact attack resource, so confirmation automatically receives every normal attack modifier plus confirmation-only modifiers. Exact confirmation resources participate in the same atomic attack registration/removal rollback.
- `Strikes` registers the typed `attack.criticalAdjustments` collection for non-additive critical-profile changes. A `CriticalAdjustment` targets an attack resource and describes a threat-range multiplier, direct threat minimum, critical-multiplier increase, or direct multiplier value using an expression; multiplier increases may carry a maximum, and every adjustment may be conditional. Exact adjustments prevent their strike from being removed until the adjustment is removed.
- `StrikesView` exposes every calculated strike. Each strike has one attack bonus combining BAB, the mode's default attack-roll ability (Strength for melee, Dexterity for ranged), the inherited attack modifier set, and the default -5 penalty when its natural classification is secondary. No iterative or repeated attacks are generated at this layer.
- `StrikeCalculationContext` carries the currently active situational condition names and optional usage overrides. `Strikes::toView(context)` and `CharacterSheet::toView(context)` calculate a coherent combined result across modifier stacking, usage, critical profile, damage dice and bonuses, distance, and usability; the no-argument overload preserves the permanent/default-usage snapshot. `StrikesView` echoes the normalized active conditions while retaining the individual conditional previews used by the GUI.
- Each `StrikeView` exposes the stable `grantId` needed by future routine selectors, effective `StrikeUsage`, effective natural classification, confirmation total and modifiers, resolved critical profiles, every resolved critical adjustment, its explicit base damage ability and effective `DamageAbilityRule`, normal/critical flat damage bonuses, and all intrinsic plus applicable externally granted damage components. Component snapshots expose dice numerically and as `XdY`, provenance, types/traits, normal inclusion, and critical occurrences calculated from the context-effective multiplier.
- Threat-range multipliers use only the greatest applicable multiplier, matching the non-stacking rule for effects such as Keen and Improved Critical; direct threat minima use the most favorable threshold. Critical multiplier increases accumulate and declared caps constrain the final result. Multiple identical multiplier replacements coexist, while conflicting replacement values are rejected instead of selecting the highest. Individual conditional profiles and the combined active-condition profile are both available.
- Components can register `AttackAbilityReplacement` and `DamageAbilityReplacement` entries through separate typed collections. Each replacement has stable identity, provenance, an attack/damage hierarchy target, and an ability; it applies to the target and every descendant. Exact replacements prevent removal of their attack until the dependency is removed.
- `StrikeView` never selects an ability replacement. It exposes a calculated base attack-ability option plus every applicable replacement, each with its own single-strike bonus and confirmation total; damage follows the same pattern, preserving the strike's `DamageAbilityRule` while recalculating the ability contribution and normal/critical flat bonuses.
- Weapon strikes declare an intrinsic `WeaponWeight` (`Light`, `OneHanded`, or `TwoHanded`). Components can register `StrikeWeaponWeightAdjustment` entries through `strike.weaponWeightAdjustments`, targeting an attack resource and either the general classification or only a particular rules purpose. General adjustments feed every purpose; purpose-specific adjustments leave the general classification unchanged. When several classifications apply, the lightest one wins, modeling effects that make a weapon count as a lighter category. `StrikeView` exposes the base, general-effective, and two-weapon-fighting-effective classifications plus full adjustment provenance.
- Components can register `StrikeUsageAdjustment` entries through `strike.usageAdjustments`. An entry targets an attack-resource subtree and one usage, and may replace that usage's attack penalty through an expression and/or its damage-ability rule. The most favorable applicable attack penalty wins; conflicting damage-rule replacements are rejected. An adjustment for `NaturalSecondary` also applies to intrinsically secondary natural strikes in their default usage, allowing Multiattack to replace the normal -5 penalty with -2 without naming the feat in strike calculation.
- `AttackRoutine` is an immutable structural template registered through the typed `attackRoutine.grants` collection. `AttackRoutines` owns the registered templates, depends on `Strikes` for current candidate metadata, and is owned by `CharacterSheet` immediately after `Strikes`. No universal routine is invented at initialization yet; concrete base-rule, class, feat, race, or effect components will register them.
- Every routine contains ordered `RoutineSlot` values. A slot has a structural `StrikeSelector`, `ChooseOne` or `AllMatching` selection mode, required `StrikeUsage`, one or more attack progressions, and an optional future damage-ability-rule override. Selectors can constrain allowed attack modes, require or forbid tags, and optionally whitelist stable strike grant IDs; contradictory and duplicate selector fields are rejected.
- `RoutineAttackProgression` supports fixed counts from an expression and BAB iteratives. Each progression also carries an attack-bonus-adjustment expression. `AttackRoutinesView` resolves fixed repetitions and BAB offsets such as `0/-5/-10` without applying them to a selected strike; a slot may combine a fixed progression with one BAB progression to represent patterns such as flurry of blows.
- Every routine slot also has a shared base attack-bonus-adjustment expression, distinct from the per-attack offsets in its progressions. Components can add `RoutineProgressionGrant` entries through `attackRoutine.progressionGrants`, so features such as Improved or Greater Two-Weapon Fighting extend an existing off-hand slot with additional attacks at -5/-10 instead of replacing the routine.
- Components can add stackable `RoutineAttackBonusAdjustment` entries through `attackRoutine.attackBonusAdjustments`. They may affect one slot or every slot in a routine and may require an assigned strike in another slot with a particular grant ID, tag, or effective weapon weight for a declared purpose. `AttackRoutinesView` exposes the unresolved assignment requirements and resolved numeric values; `AttacksView` evaluates them against the attack's effective slot assignments and exposes the base/effective shared adjustment plus every applied entry. This models the two-weapon-fighting table: intrinsic -6/-10 penalties, +2 to both when the off-hand weapon counts as light, and the feat's +2/+6 corrections compose to -2/-2.
- `AttackRoutinesView` exposes every routine and slot definition plus every currently registered strike as a candidate. Each candidate carries its grant ID, name, usage channels, acceptance flag, and Italian rejection reasons calculated from the selector and required usage. This gives the GUI enough information to filter structurally invalid choices while retaining rejected entries for explanation.
- `Attack` is user-owned configuration state created directly by `Attacks`, without a public `AttackDefinition`. It stores normalized ID, display name, selected routine ID, and only explicit `ChooseOne` assignments as `slotId -> strikeGrantId`. `CharacterSheet` exposes `createAttack`, `removeAttack`, `assignStrikeToAttack`, and `unassignStrikeFromAttack`; creation requires a currently registered routine, assignment requires a compatible registered candidate, and explicit assignments with overlapping usage channels are rejected.
- `AllMatching` slots have no stored assignments. `AttacksView` derives their effective grant IDs on every snapshot from the routine's current candidates, removes candidates whose usage channels conflict with explicit assignments, and resolves collisions among automatic candidates deterministically in strike registration order. Removing an explicit assignment therefore makes compatible automatic strikes reappear without mutating the attack. Each slot retains all candidates with Italian conflict explanations, its effective IDs, completion/usability, selector, usage, and progressions.
- A configured attack remains persisted if its routine, slot, or assigned strike later disappears. Its View becomes incomplete and unusable with a reason instead of silently deleting user state. `AttackData` stores identity, name, routine ID, and explicit assignments; `AttacksData` is included in the sheet save root. Derived `AllMatching` membership and calculated routine output are never saved.
- `SkillType` defines the standard Pathfinder skills and their fixed metadata: display label, resource name, default ability, trained-only status, specialization requirement, and armor-check-penalty applicability.
- `Skill` models one concrete skill entry, including specialized Craft/Perform/Profession entries, and registers only an enhanceable resource. Skills are deliberately not expression targets; each calculated option combines its ability modifier, ranks, the trained class-skill bonus, and the skill's `ResourceManager` modifiers.
- `Skills` owns all non-specialized standard skills and Craft/Perform/Profession entries. It creates and registers the canonical specializations at initialization and allows additional user-defined specializations. Canonical specializations remain present in the model; eventual visibility filtering belongs to the GUI.
- Components may register `SkillAbilityReplacement` entries through `skill.abilityReplacements`, identifying an ID, source, target skill resource, and replacement ability. A replacement applies to its exact target and every descendant in the skill resource hierarchy, so category replacements automatically cover existing and future specializations. Duplicate IDs, missing resources, and non-skill targets are rejected.
- Components grant class-skill status through `SkillClassSkillGrant` entries registered in `skill.classSkillGrants`. Each grant records a stable ID, source, and target skill resource, and applies to that resource and all descendants; a category grant therefore covers existing and future specializations. Duplicate IDs, missing resources, and non-skill targets are rejected.
- `SkillView` exposes identity, display name, derived class-skill status and bonus, every applicable class-skill grant with provenance, ranks, usability, resolved modifiers, whether the entry is custom, and a fully calculated default ability option plus every applicable replacement. The backend never selects an ability option; the GUI chooses which valid result to display. `SkillsView` provides every concrete skill in deterministic type/specialization order and is included in `CharacterSheetView`.
- `SkillSaveData` stores only user-owned source state for one concrete skill: identity, custom specialization metadata, and ranks. Class-skill grants, ability associations, and component-owned replacements are reconstructed rather than persisted. `SkillsSaveData` stores the complete deterministic skill collection; loading recreates custom resources before modifier groups and validates built-in completeness, identity uniqueness, and canonical/custom classification.
- `SavingThrow` models Fortitude, Reflex, or Will with its structural default ability: Constitution, Dexterity, or Wisdom. `SavingThrows` owns all three and registers `savingThrow.all` plus the concrete enhanceable resources so global and per-save modifiers compose through inheritance. Each save also owns an accumulated `savingThrow.<type>.base` resource; class and other structural contributions build its base bonus automatically.
- Components may register independent `SavingThrowAbilityReplacement` entries through `savingThrow.abilityReplacements`, identifying their stable ID, source, affected save, and replacement ability. Duplicate IDs are rejected. The backend never selects or persists an alternative: each `SavingThrowView` contains the fully calculated default option and every registered replacement, while the GUI chooses which valid result to display.
- `SavingThrowView` also exposes the resolved base value, its complete contribution set, and common permanent/conditional modifier information. `SavingThrowSaveData` and `SavingThrowsSaveData` no longer exist because base values, default abilities, and replacements are reconstructed by components. `CharacterSheet` exposes no saving-throw mutation.
- `Initiative` registers the `initiative` enhanceable resource and uses Dexterity as its structural option. Components may register independent `InitiativeAbilityReplacement` entries through `initiative.abilityReplacements`; `InitiativeView` exposes the fully calculated Dexterity option and every replacement, while the backend never selects or persists one. `InitiativeSaveData` no longer exists because initiative has no user-owned source state.
- `ArmorClass` calculates normal, touch, and flat-footed AC from a base of 10, Dexterity, and inherited modifier resources. Positive ability modifiers are capped by the optional maximum Dexterity bonus for normal and touch AC; flat-footed AC removes a positive modifier but preserves a negative one.
- Armor-class modifier applicability is represented by a resource graph: `armorClass.all` feeds every variant, `armorClass.reflexive` feeds normal and touch AC, `armorClass.solid` feeds normal and flat-footed AC, and each concrete variant has its own resource for exceptional effects. Calculated armor-class values are not expression targets because there is currently no concrete consumer for them.
- Maximum Dexterity is modeled as a typed collection of independent limits rather than one mutable value. Armor, shield, load, and other sources add `MaximumDexterityLimit` entries through `armorClass.maxDexLimits`; no entries means no limit, and the effective maximum is the lowest active resolved limit.
- Each maximum-Dexterity category inherits from `armorClass.maxDex.all`, while every dynamic `armorClass.maxDex.limit.<id>` resource inherits from its category. Normal modifiers can therefore adjust every limit, only armor/shield/load/other limits, or one exact source. Removing a limit is rejected while its exact resource still contains modifiers. Limit results are clamped to zero.
- Components may register independent `ArmorClassAbilityReplacement` entries through `armorClass.abilityReplacements`. The backend never selects or persists one: `ArmorClassView` always exposes the complete Dexterity-based option plus one fully calculated option per registered replacement, and the GUI decides which valid option to display. Duplicate replacement IDs are rejected and removing a missing entry fails.
- Components may register `ArmorClassAbilitySuppression` entries through `armorClass.abilitySuppressions`. Any active suppression removes only the positive contribution of the CA ability slot after ordinary maximum-Dexterity limits are resolved, while preserving negative modifiers. Suppressions are structural and cannot be weakened by modifiers that raise a maximum-Dexterity limit; they also affect replacement abilities occupying that slot but do not affect independent CA modifiers such as a Monk's `+@wisMod`. Multiple sources coexist by stable ID, and the View exposes the active flag and every source.
- `ArmorClassView` also exposes the effective maximum Dexterity bonus and every source limit with its base/effective values and modifiers. Each ability option contains its raw ability contribution and separate resolved entries for normal, touch, and flat-footed AC. There is no `ArmorClassSaveData`: substitutions and limits are reconstructed by their owning class, talent, equipment, load, or effect component.
- `CharacterSheet` owns `ArmorClass` and includes it in GUI snapshots, but deliberately exposes no operation for users to change its defensive ability, register arbitrary substitutions, or directly set maximum-Dexterity limits. Uncanny-dodge behavior remains a subsequent armor-class step.
- `HitPoints` stores base maximum, lethal damage taken, temporary hit points, and non-lethal damage. Its effective maximum adds the accumulated `hp.max` contributions and never drops below zero; current hit points are derived as `max - damageTaken`, so maximum changes automatically adjust current hit points while preserving wounds. `hp.max` is an accumulated resource, while none of the hit-point values are expression targets.
- `HitPoints` installs its structural `hitPoints.constitution` contribution on `hp.max` with expression `@conMod * @level` when it registers the resource. `CharacterSheet` currently supplies the provisional `level` target, which always resolves to 1. The contribution is rebuilt at startup and is not persisted; character level is still a temporary approximation for Hit Dice, and racial Hit Dice plus the minimum of 1 hit point per Hit Die remain future concerns.
- `HitPoints::damage` consumes temporary hit points first, then applies lethal damage to current hit points or accumulates non-lethal damage up to the effective maximum and converts overflow to lethal damage. `HitPoints::heal` restores current hit points up to the effective maximum and removes the same amount of non-lethal damage without restoring temporary hit points.
- Hit-point damage application uses `DamageLethality` (`Lethal` or `NonLethal`), keeping that concern distinct from the attack model's `DamageType` categories.
- Temporary hit points are stored as independent source pools rather than one integer. Each pool has a user-provided normalized ID, remaining points, and an optional declared `GameDuration`; different IDs stack, while reapplying the same ID keeps the stronger remaining amount and, on a tie, the longer declared duration. Damage consumes pools by shortest declared duration first, indefinite pools last, with IDs breaking ties deterministically. Removing a pool discards its remaining points without transferring damage.
- Temporary-hit-point duration is metadata only: it is exposed in the View and SaveData but is never decremented and never causes autonomous expiration. `CharacterSheet::advanceTime` does not exist. A future `TimeManager` will own actual game-time advancement and let components register themselves or reversible expiration actions; an expiring source will then remove its temporary-hit-point grant through the existing collection protocol.
- `GameDuration` remains a small immutable value object that represents rounds and provides checked factories for rounds, minutes, and hours. New JSON writes the temporary-pool metadata as `durationRounds`; legacy `remainingRounds` values are accepted as equivalent metadata when loading older saves.
- `HitPointsView` exposes all four sheet values, individual temporary-hit-point pools, and the resolved maximum contribution set. `HitPointsSaveData` persists internal base maximum, damage taken, temporary pools, and non-lethal damage, while calculated maximum/current totals are derived and structural contributions are reconstructed by their owning components. `CharacterSheet` exposes only healing, typed damage, and time advancement as user-facing hit-point interactions. Temporary pools are added and removed internally through the `hp.temporary` collection by their owning components.
- The first modifier-domain slice is implemented: modifier enums, stacking metadata, immutable modifier data, UUID generation, normalization, and constructor invariants.
- `BonusType::Generic` represents bonuses without a more specific Pathfinder type. Generic bonuses always stack.
- `ResourceManager` owns the `ModifierSet` of every registered enhanceable resource and keeps live integer targets in a separate normalized registry. It routes modifier additions/removals, calculates totals by resource name, and detects circular target resolution.
- `ResourceManager` supports typed collection resources as its third resource category. A component registers a normalized resource name, its item type, and add/remove callbacks; callers add strongly typed values through the global manager, while an incompatible item type is rejected before dispatch. Collection names remain available through the manager's diagnostic `ResourceManagerView`, which is not exposed by `CharacterSheetView`.
- `Movement` registers `movement.grants` for `MovementGrant` values, `movement.adjustments` for `MovementAdjustment` values, and `movement.runAdjustments` for `RunAdjustment` values. `HitPoints` registers `hp.temporary` for `TemporaryHitPointGrant` values; the grant amount is an integer expression resolved through the manager when added. The component remains the owner of its state and rules, while `ResourceManager` only validates and routes collection operations.
- `Movement` has no save root: grants, movement adjustments, and run adjustments are reconstructed by their owning race, class, item, condition, or effect component. `CharacterSheet` does not invent a default racial grant. Hit-point persistence still stores mutable remaining temporary pools. The collection registry is always reconstructed at startup.
- `ResourceManager` also supports accumulated resources backed by `ContributionSet`. Each `Contribution` has an explicit normalized ID and an integer expression; all contributions stack, may resolve to signed values, and can be added or removed independently without modifier types, conditions, or stacking rules. `HitPoints` uses this mechanism for its effective maximum.
- Enhanceable resources may inherit from already registered parent resources. Totals and views combine direct and inherited modifiers before applying stacking rules, deduplicate shared ancestors in multiple-inheritance graphs, and automatically expose category modifiers to resources registered later.
- Skill modifiers use a resource hierarchy rooted at `skill.all`. `skill.craft`, `skill.perform`, `skill.profession`, `skill.knowledge`, and `skill.armorCheckPenalty` inherit from that root. Craft/Perform/Profession specializations inherit from their respective category, Knowledge skills inherit from `skill.knowledge`, armor-check-penalty skills inherit from `skill.armorCheckPenalty`, and every other concrete skill inherits directly from `skill.all`. Consequently global and category modifiers automatically reach existing and future descendants without each skill listing all applicable categories.
- `Skills` also registers the independent semantic resources `skillCheck.str`, `skillCheck.dex`, `skillCheck.con`, `skillCheck.int`, `skillCheck.wis`, and `skillCheck.cha`. Each ability option resolves its ordinary skill-resource hierarchy together with the semantic resource for the ability it currently uses, applying stacking rules once across both sets. Ability replacements therefore automatically gain or lose ability-based skill-check effects. `SkillAbilityOptionView` exposes this combined `ModifierSetView`, while the top-level skill modifier view remains the common skill-only snapshot.
- Integer expressions support literals, registered targets prefixed by `@`, parentheses, unary signs, whitespace, the four basic arithmetic operators with integer division, comparisons, and the logical operators `!`, `&&`, and `||`; logical results are represented as 0 or 1.
- `Requirement` is a reusable expression plus a GUI-facing failure reason. A `Modifier` may own multiple requirements, all of which must resolve to nonzero before it participates in permanent or conditional stacking. Inapplicable modifiers remain in `ModifierView` with their resolved value, active state, and individual requirement results.
- `ModifierSet` calculates permanent totals through `ResourceManager`, applies Pathfinder stacking rules, caps inherent bonuses at +5, subtracts all penalties, excludes conditional modifiers, and rejects negative resolved magnitudes.
- `ModifierView` exposes every modifier field plus its resolved value. `ModifierSetView` contains both `permanentTotal` and the context-effective `total`, effective single-condition deltas grouped by condition, and all resolved modifier snapshots. `ResourceManager::modifierSetView(resource, activeConditions)` resolves all simultaneous conditions with the normal stacking rules while evaluating each expression only once per snapshot.
- `AbilityView` contains the ability type, base value, total value, derived modifier, and its complete `ModifierSetView`; `AbilityScore::toView` resolves the modifier set once per snapshot.
- `AbilitySaveData` contains only the ability type and base value; calculated values and modifier snapshots are rebuilt rather than persisted.
- `ContributionView` contains the ID, expression, and resolved value; `ContributionSetView` adds the accumulated total. Group and manager views expose targeted contributions and deterministically ordered group states.
- There is no persistence data for modifiers, requirements, contributions, movement grants, movement adjustments, or registered resource sets. These are structural objects reconstructed by their owning model components; only their resolved GUI views remain.
- `CharacterSheetSaveData` is the versioned persistence root and contains ability, hit-point, skill, configured-attack, and manual-condition source data; armor class, initiative, saving throws, structural strikes/routines, `ResourceManager`, modifiers, contributions, movement, condition definitions, source-controlled condition entries, derived entries, and applied condition effects have no direct save data because their state is rebuilt by model components. The current format version is 18; versions 8, 10, 11, 12, 13, 14, 15, 16, and 17 remain loadable, with absent attacks and pre-version-17 conditions interpreted as empty and version-17 condition entries interpreted as having no parameter. Obsolete modifier, contribution, and movement-group fields in older JSON are ignored, while legacy temporary-hit-point `remainingRounds` is retained as duration metadata. The unreleased version 9 movement-root experiment is intentionally rejected because it could not distinguish structural from user-owned grants.
- The separate `golarion_persistence` library converts `CharacterSheetSaveData` to and from readable JSON through `nlohmann-json`; persistence enum names are stable English identifiers and do not reuse GUI labels.
- `CharacterSheet::save(path)` delegates JSON file writing to the persistence module and reports file opening or writing failures with exceptions.
- `CharacterSheet::load(path)` validates format version and the complete set of six abilities, restores user-owned source values, and lets model components reconstruct all structural modifiers, contributions, grants, and adjustments. Armor-class substitutions and limits are not loaded from JSON.
- Manual modifier, contribution, and movement groups, their managers, GUI views, save data, JSON sections, sheet APIs, and tests have been removed. The autonomous model currently has no extension point for house rules; concrete components interact directly with `ResourceManager`.
- `ResourceManagerView` remains a diagnostic snapshot used by focused manager and resource-hierarchy tests. It exposes registered targets, collections, and enhanceable resources with their resolved modifiers, but is deliberately not part of the GUI-facing sheet snapshot.
- `CharacterSheetView` aggregates abilities, strikes, structural attack routines, configured attacks, hit points, initiative, armor class, saving throws, skills, resolved movement, and the condition-manager snapshot for the GUI.
- `CharacterSheet` owns `ConditionManager` and registers all 30 canonical character-condition definitions currently represented after every model component has installed its resources. The sheet exposes `addCondition`/`removeCondition` as the manual GUI channel; automatic component-owned entries still use `condition.entries`. `ConditionManager` owns definitions and active entries, validates references and duplicate IDs, and exposes active/count/origin queries plus View and persistence snapshots. Every definition has one or more ordered, uniquely identified stages, and every stage may declaratively derive other conditions at a positive severity and declare reversible effects. A definition may declare one required textual entry parameter: the View exposes its Italian label, every entry exposes its value, and manual values survive save/load; entries missing a required parameter or supplying one to an incompatible condition are rejected.
- Condition activation has two physically separate channels. `ConditionManager::addManualEntry` creates GUI/table-owned entries that only `removeManualEntry` may remove; additions through the `condition.entries` collection are automatically source-controlled and only collection removal may remove them. Callers cannot choose or spoof `ConditionEntryOrigin`; `Derived` is assigned only by the internal dependency resolver. `effectiveSeverity` resolves `Shared` conditions to 1 while active, sums `PerEntry` severity as a diagnostic total without collapsing the independently stored entries, and resolves `Escalating` conditions by summing independent sources plus the greatest contributor in each stacking group, then taking the greater result against every non-contributing minimum severity. Progressive calculation saturates directly at the final available stage, so no unused uncapped severity is retained or exposed.
- Derived conditions are reconciled transitively from all cumulatively active stages into deterministic internal entries with `Derived` origin. Reconciliation starts from manual and source-controlled entries, computes the complete desired closure in temporary state, and commits only on success; unknown active targets, ID conflicts, or invalid graphs therefore cannot leave partial state. Derived entries cannot be removed through either external channel, disappear with their originating stage, preserve independent target sources, and circular derivation graphs are rejected when the closing definition is registered.
- A stage effect has a stable ID, an Italian GUI description, and an apply callback that receives `ResourceManager` plus a stable effect-instance context and returns its exact cleanup callback. Shared and escalating effects exist once per active stage, while `PerEntry` effects have a separate instance for every entry. The manager diffs desired and applied instances, keeps unchanged effects alive, applies only newly reached stages, and cleans only stages or entries that disappeared. A failed application cleans every effect created by that attempted transition and leaves the entry state unchanged; cleanup callbacks have an explicit no-throw contract. Destroying the manager cleans remaining effects and unregisters its collection resource.
- `ConditionManagerView` exposes every definition in deterministic ID order, its resolved severity, ordered stages and activation state, declared derivations, effect descriptions and active effect instances, plus every active entry with provenance, stacking data, and origin. `ConditionManagerSaveData` contains only manual entries; source-controlled entries are recreated by their owning sheet components, derived entries are reconciled from active stages, and effects are reapplied from definitions. Loading all manual entries is one atomic manager transition.
- Every canonical definition has its own implementation file under `core/src/character/conditions`; shared effect factories create modifiers, contributions, replacements, suppressions, defense alternatives, and movement adjustments with exact reversible cleanup. All definition files are compiled and registered through `registerCanonicalConditions`. A smoke test activates and removes every definition, including the maximum stages of Paura and Affaticamento.
- `AbilityChecks` registers `abilityCheck.all` and one descendant for each ability. `AbilityView` now exposes the calculated raw ability-check total and its modifiers separately from the ability score and modifier, so check penalties never alter the underlying score. Initiative inherits from `abilityCheck.all`, correctly treating initiative as an ability check even when another ability replaces Dexterity.
- `Strikes` registers `damage.weaponRoll` beneath `damage.all`. The weapon, natural, and unarmed damage categories inherit from it, while generic ranged, thrown, and projectile categories do not; a thrown weapon still inherits it through its simultaneous weapon tag. This lets effects modify weapon damage rolls once without affecting unrelated damage.
- The canonical `sickened`/`Infermo` definition is registered only after every sheet component has installed its resources. Its five reversible actions apply untyped penalties of 2 to `attack.all`, `damage.weaponRoll`, `savingThrow.all`, `skill.all`, and `abilityCheck.all`, covering attack rolls, weapon damage rolls, saving throws, skill checks, and ability checks. Multiple sources still apply this package only once, and removing the last source cleans up every generated modifier.
- The canonical `fear`/`Paura` definition has three cumulative stages: `shaken`/`Scosso`, `frightened`/`Spaventato`, and `panicked`/`In preda al panico`. Scosso applies reversible penalties of 2 to attack rolls, saving throws, skill checks, and ability checks. The behavioral rules of the higher stages remain for the future action/reminder systems rather than being represented as fake no-op effects. Two ordinary Scosso sources escalate to Spaventato; a severity-2 source plus an independent Scosso source escalates to In preda al panico. Repeated applications from the same effect can share a `stackingGroup`, and exceptional effects can set `contributesToEscalation = false`.
- The canonical `fatigue`/`Affaticamento` definition has two cumulative stages. `fatigued`/`Affaticato` applies penalties of 2 to `str` and `dex`. `exhausted`/`Esausto` adds another 4 points of penalty to both abilities and a 50% `MovementAdjustment` matching every movement grant, producing total penalties of 6 and half speed. Downgrading removes only the second-stage effects. Entries use the same severity, escalation, and stacking-key policy as Paura, including non-escalating fatigue sources.
- The canonical `dazzled`/`Abbagliato` definition applies a penalty of 1 to `attack.all` and a conditional penalty of 1 to `skill.perception` under the Italian condition label `Basate sulla vista`. Its Perception penalty is therefore absent from the permanent total, visible as a conditional result to the GUI, and included when that calculation context is active.
- The canonical `entangled`/`Intralciato` definition applies penalties of 2 to `attack.all` and 4 to `dex`, plus a 50% `MovementAdjustment` matching every movement grant. All three effects are reversible and removed with the final condition entry.
- The canonical `blinded`/`Accecato` definition applies a penalty of 2 to `armorClass.all`, registers an `ArmorClassAbilitySuppression`, and adds conditional penalties of 4 to `skillCheck.str`, `skillCheck.dex`, and opposed `skill.perception` checks. Removing its final entry reverses every registered effect. Automatic failure of sight-dependent checks, opponents' total concealment, and the Acrobatics rule for moving faster than half speed remain deferred to the future GUI-facing `Reminder` system rather than being approximated numerically.
- The canonical `cowering`/`Accovacciato` definition applies a penalty of 2 to `armorClass.all` and registers an `ArmorClassAbilitySuppression`; both effects are removed with its final entry. Its inability to act remains deferred to `ActionManager`. `Paura` still describes becoming Accovacciato when trapped instead of creating the entry automatically, because the model cannot determine whether escape is possible.
- The canonical `fascinated`/`Affascinato` definition applies a conditional penalty of 4 to `skill.all` for checks required as reactions. Its inability to act remains deferred to `ActionManager`; the new saving throw caused by a potential threat, automatic interruption caused by an obvious threat, and an ally shaking the creature free remain deferred to the future `Reminder` area.
- The canonical `deafened`/`Assordato` definition applies a penalty of 4 to `initiative`. Automatic failure of sound-based Perception checks, the 20% failure chance for spells with verbal components, and possible long-term adaptation at the GM's discretion remain deferred to the future `Reminder` area.
- The canonical `staggered`/`Barcollante` definition has no provisional actions. Its per-round restriction to either one standard or one move action, prohibition on full-round actions, and continued availability of free, swift, and immediate actions belong entirely to the future `ActionManager`. `HitPoints` must later create and remove its source-controlled entry when non-lethal damage equals current hit points.
- The canonical `confused`/`Confuso` definition has no actions. Its complete rule package—including the per-turn percentile table, target selection, retaliation, self-inflicted damage, beneficial touch attacks, and attack-of-opportunity behavior—is deferred to the future GUI-facing `Reminder` area rather than `ActionManager`, because resolving it depends on table and scene state outside the character sheet.
- The canonical `dazed`/`Frastornato` definition has no provisional actions. Its inability to act belongs to the future `ActionManager`; its usually one-round duration is owned by the applying source and later by `TimeManager`. It intentionally applies no armor-class penalty.
- The canonical `flatFooted`/`Impreparato` definition reversibly suppresses positive ability bonuses in armor-class calculation and positive Dexterity in CMD. Its inability to make attacks of opportunity remains deferred to `ActionManager`.
- The canonical `grappled`/`In Lotta` definition reversibly applies a penalty of 4 to Dexterity and a penalty of 2 to `attack.all`. Because BMC inherits the attack hierarchy, it also installs a stacking generic +2 on the exact Lottare BMC resource; this cancels only the condition's inherited -2 for attempts to grapple or escape while preserving any reduction caused by the lower Dexterity when a replacement ability uses it. Movement, attacks of opportunity, two-handed actions, spellcasting restrictions, and related checks remain deferred to their dedicated future systems.
- The canonical `disabled`/`Inabile` definition currently applies only a reversible 50% movement adjustment to every mode. `HitPoints` must later own its automatic source entry at exactly 0 current hit points, or at negative hit points when the character is stable and conscious. Its standard-or-move action economy belongs to `ActionManager`; the 1 damage after a strenuous action and the unaided daily recovery rules belong to the future Reminder area because the backend does not execute actions or adjudicate rest checks.
- The canonical `incorporeal`/`Incorporeo` definition deliberately has no actions. Its immunity to nonmagical attacks, half damage from magical weapons, spells, spell-like and supernatural abilities, and full damage from force or incorporeal sources are all future GUI reminders; no provisional damage-defense subsystem is introduced for them.
- The canonical `helpless`/`Indifeso` definition reversibly registers a final Dexterity replacement of 0. This produces modifier -5 and propagates through the existing `dex`/`dexMod` targets to armor class, CMD, Reflex, initiative, skills, and every other consumer. The +4 received by melee attackers, sneak-attack eligibility, and coup-de-grace rules remain future GUI reminders because they describe incoming encounter interactions.
- The canonical `invisible`/`Invisibile` definition registers two reversible, situational effects under the shared GUI context `Contro avversari che non percepiscono l'attaccante`: a stacking generic +2 on `attack.all` and a flat-footed armor-class defense alternative targeting every strike. Without the context, the bonus remains visible but does not alter the ordinary attack total; the defense alternative always carries its applicability label for GUI selection. Visual detection, concealment, and encounter-specific perception remain future reminders, while contextual CMD and Uncanny Dodge interactions remain deferred to their dedicated systems.
- The canonical `negativeLevels`/`Livelli Negativi` definition uses `PerEntry` application: every entry represents exactly one negative level and independently applies stacking penalties of 1 to `attack.all`, `savingThrow.all`, `skill.all`, `abilityCheck.all`, and `combatManeuver.defense.all`, plus a -5 contribution to `hp.max`. BMC inherits its penalty from `attack.all`; initiative and ability-bound checks inherit from `abilityCheck.all`. Hit points keep damage taken as their source state, so reducing maximum hit points also lowers current hit points by 5 and removing that exact entry restores both values without healing damage. Reducing effective level for level-dependent variables, death when negative levels reach total Hit Dice, and temporary-level daily saving throws remain deferred until level/Hit-Dice, death, time, and reminder systems exist. The global `level` target is intentionally not reduced, because doing so would incorrectly recalculate structural level-scaled contributions such as Constitution to maximum hit points.
- The canonical `dying`/`Morente` definition declaratively derives `Privo di Sensi`. `HitPoints` must later apply Morente while current hit points are negative and the character is not stable; the Constitution stabilization check, loss of 1 hit point on failure, and death threshold remain deferred to the future reminder, turn/time, stability, and death systems. Inability to act belongs to `Privo di Sensi` and the future `ActionManager`, not to a duplicate Morente restriction.
- The canonical `dead`/`Morto` definition currently has no actions. `HitPoints` must later create its source-controlled entry when current hit points reach negative Constitution; ability and effect components may own independent entries when Constitution reaches 0 or an effect kills outright. Inability to act belongs to `ActionManager`, blocking ordinary and magical healing belongs to the future healing system, and resurrection plus corpse decay belong to future resurrection and reminder systems.
- The canonical `nauseated`/`Nauseato` definition currently has no actions. Its restriction to one move action per turn and its prohibition against attacking, casting or concentrating on spells, and performing other attention-demanding tasks belong entirely to the future `ActionManager`; it does not numerically reduce movement speed.
- The canonical `paralyzed`/`Paralizzato` definition reversibly installs a final Strength replacement of 0, blocks every movement grant through a global `MovementAdjustment`, and declaratively derives `Indifeso`. Indifeso supplies the final Dexterity replacement of 0 without duplicating it. Removing Paralizzato restores Strength and movement and removes only its derived Indifeso source, preserving any independent source that still makes the character helpless. Restriction to mental actions belongs to `ActionManager`; falling while flying and drowning risk while swimming belong to the future Reminder area.
- The canonical `petrified`/`Pietrificato` definition declaratively derives `Privo di Sensi`. The integrity and completeness of the stone body and any permanent consequences when returning to flesh belong to the future Reminder area rather than a provisional damage model.
- The canonical `unconscious`/`Privo di Sensi` definition declaratively derives `Indifeso`, which supplies the final Dexterity replacement of 0. Multiple independent unconscious sources therefore share the helpless effects and removing one source cannot clear the others. Inability to act belongs to the future `ActionManager`; `HitPoints` must later own a direct source entry when non-lethal damage exceeds current hit points.
- The canonical `prone`/`Prono` definition applies a permanent penalty of 4 to melee attack rolls and exposes two contextual armor-class results: -4 against melee attacks and a stacking generic +4 against ranged attacks. The ordinary armor-class total remains unchanged until the GUI or another caller evaluates the corresponding incoming-attack context. Prohibiting ranged weapons other than crossbows and standing as a move action that provokes attacks of opportunity belong to the future `ActionManager`, which will need strike semantics capable of identifying crossbows.
- The rules condition `Rotto` is intentionally not registered in the character `ConditionManager`: it belongs to individual weapons, armor, shields, tools, wands, and staves, and its behavior depends on the affected object's category. Implement it with the future equipment/item model, where it can alter that object's attacks, damage, critical profile, armor contribution, skill use, charges, repair rules, and value without pretending that the whole character is broken.
- The canonical `bleeding`/`Sanguinante` definition is registered as a reminder-only condition with the required textual entry parameter `Danno per turno`, allowing values such as `2d6 PF`, `1 Forza`, or an equivalent complete description. Every source remains independently visible and manual sources are saved. The backend deliberately does not apply damage or resolve competing entries yet: effects of the same damage kind select the worst result while different kinds coexist, which belongs to the future `Reminder`/time pipeline once turn events and ability damage/drain exist.
- The canonical `stable`/`Stabilizzato` definition declaratively derives `Privo di Sensi`; later `HitPoints` integration will own the source-controlled Stabilizzato entry while the character has negative hit points and is stable.
- The canonical `stunned`/`Stordito` definition reversibly applies a penalty of 2 to `armorClass.all` and suppresses the positive ability bonus to armor class. Dropping held objects and inability to act remain deferred to the future equipment and action systems.
- The canonical `pinned`/`Immobilizzato` definition applies and cleans up its separate penalty of 4 to `armorClass.all`, its own armor-class ability suppression, and declaratively derives `Impreparato`. Its independent suppression ensures that a future Schivare Prodigioso exception cannot preserve the armor-class bonus while Immobilizzato. Cleanup removes only the derived entry and therefore preserves independent sources of `Impreparato`. Inability to move and other action limitations belong to the future `ActionManager`; escape attempts, spell-component limitations, and its Concentration check remain future reminders. `In Lotta` and `Immobilizzato` stay independent, with grapple-state transitions owned by their applying component.
- A minimal C-compatible API creates and destroys opaque character-sheet handles.
- Further C API design is deferred until the C++ model is complete; current model additions are not exposed through FFI.
- CMake builds the model, shared interoperability library, and smoke test.
- Frontend work is explicitly deferred.
- Backend headers, implementations, and tests mirror the logical areas `character`, `resource`, `view`, `data`, `persistence`, `util`, and `ffi`; modifiers and contributions both live under `resource`, while all GUI snapshot structs are collected under `include/golarion/view` and use the `View` suffix.

## Important Decisions

- The domain model is implemented in modern C++.
- A thin API with C linkage will be the only boundary exposed to a future frontend through FFI.
- C++ types, STL containers, ownership, and exceptions do not cross that boundary.
- Complex frontend snapshots may later be exchanged as JSON, but no serialization dependency is introduced yet.
- GUI snapshots use `View`/`toView`; future persistence structures will use `SaveData`/`toSaveData` to keep calculated presentation state separate from serialized source state.
- Character-sheet components register enhanceable resources by name; they neither create nor retain their `ModifierSet`.
- Automatic condition generation from independent character-sheet state is deliberately limited to `Barcollante`, `Privo di Sensi`, `Inabile`, `Morente`, `Stabilizzato`, and `Morto`. A condition definition may still generate another condition when the latter is a mandatory rules consequence, such as `Privo di Sensi` producing `Indifeso` or `Immobilizzato` producing `Impreparato`; the derived entry exists only while its source condition requires it. Every other character condition requires an explicit external source entry. `Impreparato` is not generated from encounter state because no combat-state tracker is planned, while `Rotto` is excluded because it is a condition of individual objects rather than of the character.

Resolution must expand every effective grant through each slot's progressions, apply the slot's `StrikeUsage`, effective shared attack-bonus adjustment, per-attack progression offset, and damage-rule override, and return ordered calculated strikes without introducing a second selection object. Concrete base routines for a standard attack, weapon full attack, natural full attack, mixed full attack, and two-weapon fighting still need structural registration; class features such as flurry can register additional templates.
- Review the modifier fields and C++ representation together.
- Design richer C API operations only when the corresponding model behavior exists.
- Connect `HitPoints` to the completed condition registry: 0 current hit points should apply Inabile, negative hit points should apply either Morente or Stabilizzato according to stability, reaching the negative-Constitution threshold should apply Morto, non-lethal damage equal to current hit points should apply Barcollante, and non-lethal damage above current hit points should apply Privo di Sensi. The mandatory Morente/Stabilizzato → Privo di Sensi → Indifeso chains are already declarative definitions.
- Model exceptions to `Impreparato` semantically rather than removing the condition entry. `Riflessi in Combattimento` belongs to the future `ActionManager`: it raises the attacks-of-opportunity allowance by the positive Dexterity bonus and permits them while `Impreparato`, but changes neither armor class nor CMD. `Schivare Prodigioso` must preserve the positive Dexterity bonus to armor class against ordinary `Impreparato` and invisible attackers, but not against `Immobilizzato` or a successful feint; it does not preserve Dexterity in CMD. `Immobilizzato` therefore needs its own non-overridable armor-class suppression in addition to the derived `Impreparato` entry, so Schivare Prodigioso cannot incorrectly cancel that explicit exception. Finalize the generic suppression/retention protocol together before implementing these capabilities.
- Design a GUI-facing `Reminder` area for rule consequences that the backend cannot or should not automate. `Accecato` must remind the user that sight-dependent checks automatically fail, opponents have total concealment, and moving faster than half speed requires the prescribed Acrobatics check. `Incorporeo` needs its attack immunities and damage interactions; `Indifeso` needs incoming melee, sneak-attack, and coup-de-grace consequences. These must not be approximated with provisional numeric or encounter systems.
- When the proper action registry/`ActionManager` is designed, update `Affaticato`, `Esausto`, and `Intralciato` so that they make the Correre and Caricare actions unavailable with a GUI-facing reason; make `Accovacciato`, `Affascinato`, and `Frastornato` unable to act; enforce `Barcollante`'s and `Inabile`'s per-round action-economy restrictions; make `In Lotta` unable to move, make attacks of opportunity, or use actions requiring two hands; and enforce that a Sovraccarico character moves only 1.5 m as a full-round action and can do nothing else. No provisional action-restriction model should be introduced before that system exists.
- When spellcasting is modeled, make `Intralciato` require a Concentration check with DC 15 + spell level to cast; a failure loses the spell. Keep this out of the condition until a spellcasting/check resolution system exists.
- Design a dedicated `TimeManager` as the sole owner of game-time advancement. Components should register subscribers or scheduled reversible actions with it; temporary-hit-point pools may retain duration metadata for optimal damage allocation but must not advance or expire themselves. Persistence and safe unsubscription semantics must be decided when this manager is introduced.
- When `TimeManager` is implemented, revisit `TemporaryHitPoints`: replace its static declared-duration ordering with actual remaining time supplied by the manager, consume the pool whose timer expires first, keep untimed pools last, remove expired grants through the collection protocol, and move timer persistence out of the temporary-hit-point SaveData into the central time system.
- Complete movement's remaining rule pipeline when armor is modeled. Carried-load reduction now uses the official adjusted-movement table and the same idempotent adjustment type is ready for armor. Effects with a bounded increase such as `Haste` still need an explicit final-speed stage so the +9 m enhancement bonus can be capped at twice the pre-effect normal speed without bypassing stacking rules.
- When feats are modeled, `Correre` should register a +1 run multiplier adjustment and `Correre (Mitico)` an additional +2 in a distinct stacking group, producing ×5/×4 and ×7/×6 with light-or-no/heavy armor or load respectively. Keeping the Dexterity bonus to armor class while running is a separate action-context rule and remains pending.

- Decide whether conditional ability substitutions need explicit model support. This is optional: the application may instead leave situational substitutions for the player to remember and apply at the table.
